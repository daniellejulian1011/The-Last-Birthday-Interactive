# THE LAST BIRTHDAY · CINEMATIC STORY BIBLE

## High Concept

Genre: first-person psychological survival-horror visual novel / branching mystery.
Period: October 27–28, 1983, on the tenth anniversary of the Candlewick's Celebration Palace tragedy.
Core emotional question: What does it mean to prove an injustice after the people who died cannot benefit from it?
Player fantasy: Explore a dead celebration palace, recover its hidden records, survive a silent mechanical pursuer, and decide what becomes of the evidence.
Tagline: **SOME PARTIES NEVER END.**

## Grounded chronology

**1973** — Candlewick's was a colorful 1970s family celebration destination: birthday cakes, rented rooms, a short mechanical mascot performance, arcade games, party photography and themed rooms. The cheerful Mr. Morrow could deliver recorded birthday greetings and songs during the operating years. Staff safety supervisor **Ruth Hale** warned about improperly chained emergency exits; owner **Edwin Candlewick** overrode the warning for crowd control, theft prevention and operating convenience. During Robin Mercer's seventh birthday on October 27, 1973, a fire spread through the palace. Robin died. June, then 14, survived. The firm characterized the tragedy as an electrical accident and suppressed exit evidence.

**1983** — The venue has stood abandoned for a decade. June, now 24, learns that Edwin is preparing to demolish the building. She returns at night with a flashlight, film camera and a letter pointing toward the chained exits. In the abandoned building, the broken, roughly seven-foot mascot moves without explanation. Whether by ruined old machinery or something darker, it pursues her. Mr. Morrow is **entirely silent in the present-day scenes**. Edwin returns to ensure the original files do not reach investigators.

**Important:** Robin definitively died in the 1973 fire. He never appears alive in 1983. Ruth Hale is a historical witness whose writing survives; she does not physically appear in 1983. The story contains **no researcher character**, **no child-voice experiment** and **no microphone/psychological-research backstory**.

## People and visual direction

- **June Mercer (1973, 14):** Quietly protective older sister, cardigan under denim overalls, auburn ribbon and canvas sneakers. In the prologue, she is trying to make her brother happy and beginning to notice negligent safety systems.
- **June Mercer (1983, 24):** Practical red jacket over a striped sweater, cuffed jeans, boots, canvas bag, 35mm camera and flashlight. Perceptive, determined, frightened, capable of tenderness. Entire game is from her point of view.
- **Robin Mercer (1973, 7):** Curious, lively, affectionate, has a paper crown and a birthday photo. The tragedy centers his humanity instead of using him as a trick or secret adult survivor.
- **Mr. Morrow:** Once a playful pink rabbit entertainer with a fixed cartoon grin, vest and bow tie. Ten abandoned years later: cracked mask, empty black eye lenses, frayed pink fabric, exposed harness, uneven ears, large old shoes. Moves through mechanical thuds, scraping and unsettling jerks. **Does not talk, whisper, mimic or sing in 1983.**
- **Edwin Candlewick:** Older moneyed owner in patterned charcoal suit, burgundy waistcoat and polished shoes. Sardonic, image-conscious and evasive. His active choices — chained exits and concealment — are the central human wrongdoing.
- **Ruth Hale:** Historical supervisor who warned that chained exits endangered lives. Her memo and records, never a modern physical appearance, provide vital context.

## Locations / sensory palette

1. **Forecourt:** Full-moon night, cracked neon, boarded windows, wet concrete, June's parked station wagon. Rain taps against steel. Title-screen scene is also the actual exploration location.
2. **1973 birthday room:** Paper streamers, balloons, birthday cake, colorful mascots, bright overhead lights. A warm contrast to what comes later.
3. **1973 fire:** Orange smoke, obstructed exit bars, lights extinguishing mid-song. The chapter cuts before graphic injury.
4. **Lobby:** Broken welcome plaque, glass doors, lobby poster, birthday hats, water dripping from the roof.
5. **Great hall:** Central hub, tables, stopped clock at 8:41, ribbons and multiple corridors. Occasional scrape from the stage.
6. **Stage:** Quiet red curtains, altered props, a hidden employee badge, Robin's kept place setting and Mr. Morrow's shadow.
7. **Arcade:** Closed cabinets, green dot on a maintenance terminal, photo booth, prize ledger. Arcade terminal code 1027.
8. **Staff corridor:** Access-controlled backstage passage between the records, security and boiler spaces.
9. **Archive:** Cardboard boxes, inspection records, fire photos and Ruth Hale's memos.
10. **Security:** Camera monitors, owner's office key, records and power switch. Camera jump reveal: silent rabbit face fills the dead screen.
11. **Boiler:** Hot rust and bypass valves. Emergency release can reopen chained doors.
12. **Office:** Edwin's desk, cigar smoke, signed chain order, matchbox and safe. Safe code 2710.
13. **Birthday chamber:** Impossibly maintained table for Robin, seven candles, a stationary and silent Mr. Morrow, Edwin's offer, and one final decision.

## Jumpscares and audio

Audio intentionally uses a **no-voice WebAudio synthesis system**. It runs only after opt-in and layers low sustained drones, softly filtered air, UI percussion, electrical snaps and mechanical stabs. The 1973 prerecorded mascot's cheerful birthday greeting is *represented in on-screen narration*, not synthesized speech.

- **Fire memory:** sudden white screen bloom and crushing impact when the historic blaze begins.
- **Curtain reveal:** Mr. Morrow's looming silent face and metallic impact as he turns.
- **Arcade alarm:** a false code prompts an impact from the aisle.
- **Camera Three:** rabbit face appears too close to the lens; abrupt power cut.
- **Boiler hatch:** oversized dirty glove reaches through a grate.
- **Final corridor:** a large shadow and the choice to stay quiet, use the camera, or panic.

The Settings menu includes **Reduce flashes & jumpscares**, which replaces fast scare transitions and disables intense flashing events.

## Branching and endings

**What changes:** preserved objects, June's composure, accessible rooms and documents, whether June confronts Edwin, and the final menu options.

**Six major endings:**
1. The Truth Survives — submit sufficient documentary evidence; expose Edwin.
2. The Doors Open — release emergency doors; begin a public reckoning.
3. Ashes to Ashes — burn the ruin; at terrible cost to the evidence.
4. Alone in the Dark — escape without finishing the investigation.
5. The Last Guest — remain with the memorial setting until dawn.
6. The New Host — take Edwin's deal and suppress the surviving documents.

**Failure ending:** No More Candles — June runs into danger or lets Mr. Morrow catch her.

## What constitutes the current playable build

This is a self-contained visual novel with original painted-style vector environments, simple parallax, rain, composure, evidence, puzzles, audio, saves and branching routes. It is not a full free-movement 3D simulation. Expanding to fully modeled 3D characters would require a separate engine and performance/testing work; that is intentionally not represented as complete in this edition.
